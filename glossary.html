Glossary

Last updated: 3 November 2026

Plain-English meanings for the technical terms and abbreviations used on this site. Each entry links to the guide that uses it. These are general explanations, not legal or financial advice.

Acas early conciliation
A free service where Acas tries to help you and your employer reach agreement before a tribunal claim. You usually have to contact Acas first before making most claims, and starting it within the time limit usually pauses the clock. Was your redundancy fair?
Annual Allowance
The yearly limit on how much can be paid into all of your pensions before a tax charge can apply. GOV.UK gives it as £60,000 for this tax year. What happens to your workplace pension when you leave
Class 1A National Insurance
National Insurance paid by your employer, not you. GOV.UK says employers pay it on the part of a termination payment over £30,000. How redundancy pay is calculated and taxed
Collective consultation
The formal consultation your employer must carry out when making 20 or more redundancies at the same time. It must start at least 30 days before dismissals take effect for 20 to 99 jobs, and at least 45 days before for 100 or more. Was your redundancy fair?
Easy access account
A savings account where you can pay in and take money out whenever you like. The interest rate is usually variable. Where to keep cash savings
Emergency tax code
A temporary tax code, ending in W1, M1 or X, that works out your tax on one pay period at a time. It is often used when a new employer doesn’t have your details from your previous job. Emergency tax codes after redundancy
Enhanced (contractual) redundancy pay
Redundancy pay that is more than the legal minimum, set by your contract, a company policy, or a voluntary redundancy offer. Your employer cannot pay you less than the statutory amount. How redundancy pay is calculated and taxed
Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS)
The scheme that protects eligible savings if a UK bank, building society or credit union fails. The limit is £120,000 per person, per firm. Where to keep cash savings
Fixed-rate account
A savings account where you lock your money away for a set term at a fixed rate. Taking money out early is usually not allowed or comes with a penalty. Where to keep cash savings
Flexible ISA
An ISA that lets you take money out and put it back in the same tax year without using up more of your yearly allowance. Not all ISAs are flexible, so check with your provider. How the ISA allowance works
Garden leave
A period during your notice when you stay employed and are normally paid, but aren’t expected to work. Check your contract for how it handles holiday. Your final pay
Good leaver
A term used in some employee share schemes for someone whose reason for leaving, often including redundancy, lets them keep or use their share awards. What counts, and any deadlines, depend on the scheme rules, so ask for them in writing. What to check in your redundancy offer
ISA (Individual Savings Account)
A savings or investment account where the interest or gains are tax-free. GOV.UK says you can put up to £20,000 a tax year into ISAs in total, shared across all of them. How the ISA allowance works
New Style Jobseeker’s Allowance
A benefit for people who are unemployed or working fewer than 16 hours a week, based on National Insurance contributions. It isn’t means-tested, so savings don’t affect it. Benefits you may be able to claim while job hunting
Notice account
A savings account where you give a set period of notice before you can withdraw your money. Where to keep cash savings
P45
A form your employer gives you when you leave, showing your pay and tax so far in the tax year. You give it to your next employer so they can set up your tax correctly. Emergency tax codes after redundancy
P800
A tax calculation letter HMRC sends after the tax year if you paid too much or too little tax. If you overpaid, it explains how to claim the money back. Emergency tax codes after redundancy
Payment in lieu of holiday
Being paid for statutory holiday you haven’t taken. GOV.UK says the only time you can be paid instead of taking statutory leave is when you leave your job. Your final pay
Payment in lieu of notice (PILON)
Being paid instead of working your notice period. GOV.UK says you pay tax and National Insurance on any payment you receive instead of working your notice. It normally ends your employment straight away, so check the end date. Your final pay
Personal Savings Allowance
The amount of savings interest you can earn tax-free each tax year outside an ISA: £1,000 for basic rate taxpayers, £500 for higher rate taxpayers, and nothing for additional rate taxpayers. Where to keep cash savings
Post-Employment Notice Pay (PENP)
Part of a severance or enhanced redundancy payment that is treated as pay for your notice period. GOV.UK says you pay tax and National Insurance on it. How redundancy pay is calculated and taxed
Settlement agreement
A legal agreement in which you usually give up the right to bring certain claims in return for a payment. You must get independent advice before signing, and the Acas Code says you should normally be given at least 10 calendar days to consider it. What to check in your redundancy offer
Statutory redundancy pay
The legal minimum redundancy payment for employees with at least 2 years’ service. It depends on your age, years of service and weekly pay, which is capped. How redundancy pay is calculated and taxed
Suitable alternative employment
A different job your employer offers you instead of making you redundant. You have the right to a 4-week trial period in it, and if you unreasonably turn down a suitable job you may lose your statutory redundancy pay. Was your redundancy fair?
Termination payment
Any payment you receive when your job ends. GOV.UK says it can include statutory redundancy pay, holiday pay, unpaid wages and bonuses, and what you pay tax on depends on what is in it. How redundancy pay is calculated and taxed
Universal Credit
A means-tested benefit based on your household’s income and savings, which can include help with housing costs. Benefits you may be able to claim while job hunting
Voluntary redundancy
When your employer invites people to put themselves forward for redundancy, often with a bigger payment than the legal minimum. If it is accepted, it is still a redundancy, not a resignation. Voluntary or compulsory redundancy
