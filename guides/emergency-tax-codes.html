Emergency tax codes after redundancy, and how to get tax back

Last checked: 25 October 2026

The short answer

An emergency tax code is a temporary code. Your tax is worked out on that week’s or month’s pay alone, as if you were paid the same amount every week or month of the year. That can mean you pay the wrong amount.

The usual cause is starting a new job when your new employer doesn’t have your details from your previous one. Give your new employer your P45, or fill in a starter checklist if you don’t have one. If you have overpaid, you can get the tax back.

How to spot one

Look at your payslip. You are on an emergency code if your tax code ends in:

  • W1, used when you are paid weekly, for example 1257L W1
  • M1, used when you are paid monthly, for example 1257L M1
  • X, used when your pay dates vary

Some payroll software shows “NONCUM” instead. If your code doesn’t end in W1, M1, X or NONCUM, you are not on an emergency code.

Why it can happen after redundancy

When you are paid for the first time in a new job, your employer tells HMRC you have started. If they don’t have your previous income and tax details, they pay you using an emergency code. GOV.UK says this is usually temporary.

That makes your P45 worth keeping safe. Your old employer gives you one when you leave. If you didn’t get one, GOV.UK says to ask your previous employer for it.

What to do

  1. Give your new employer your P45. This gives them your details and can help take you off the emergency code.
  2. No P45? Fill in the starter checklist so your employer has what it needs to work out your tax.
  3. Check your first payslip. It is your responsibility to make sure you are paying the right amount of tax.
  4. Allow up to 35 days. HMRC usually updates your code once it has your details from your new and previous employers, and it can take up to 35 days from when you start. It sends the new code to you and your employer.
  5. Still wrong after 35 days? Follow the GOV.UK steps for updating your tax code.

One catch: if you have not paid enough tax, GOV.UK says you stay on the emergency code until you have paid the correct tax for the year.

Getting tax back

If you have paid too much tax, you can get a refund. After the tax year ends on 5 April, HMRC sends a tax calculation letter (a P800) if you paid too much or too little. Letters go out between June and March of the following tax year, and you only get one if you are employed or get a pension.

You might also get one if you were put on the wrong tax code, if you finished one job and started another and were paid by both in the same month, or if you received Jobseeker’s Allowance or Employment and Support Allowance. If you are registered for Self Assessment, your bill is adjusted automatically and you won’t get a letter.

If you think you have overpaid and no letter has come, GOV.UK has a page on how to claim a refund. Always use GOV.UK directly, and be careful with unexpected emails or texts about refunds.
