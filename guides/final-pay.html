Your final pay: notice, holiday pay and what you should receive

Last checked: 3 November 2026 · Covers Great Britain

The short answer

Your last payment should include more than your redundancy pay. It should cover your pay up to your last day, your notice, any holiday you haven’t taken, and any bonus or commission you have already earned. GOV.UK says employers must pay for untaken statutory holiday when you leave, even if you are dismissed for gross misconduct.

Ask for a written breakdown before you leave, and check your final payslip against it.

What should be in your final payment

ItemWhat to know
Pay up to your last dayIncluding any overtime or shift pay you have worked but not yet been paid for.
NoticeIf you work your notice, you are paid as normal. If you are paid instead, it is called payment in lieu of notice. See our guide to your redundancy offer.
Untaken holidayPaid when you leave. See below.
Bonus or commissionDepends on your contract. Ask whether any has been earned but not yet paid.
Redundancy paySee our guide to redundancy pay.

Part of the payment can be taxed differently from normal pay. Our guide to redundancy pay and tax explains which parts.

Holiday pay when you leave

  • Untaken statutory holiday must be paid. GOV.UK says the only time you can be paid instead of taking statutory leave, known as payment in lieu, is when you leave your job.
  • You may be able to take it in your notice period. GOV.UK says that during your notice period you may be able to take whatever is left of your statutory annual leave.
  • Check the amount. How much you are owed depends on how much of the leave year has passed and how much you have taken. The GOV.UK holiday entitlement calculator works it out.
  • Extra leave is separate. Statutory leave is 5.6 weeks a year. GOV.UK says if your employer offers more, they can agree separate arrangements for the extra, so check your contract.
  • Deductions need agreement. If you have taken more leave than you were entitled to, GOV.UK says your employer must not take money from your final pay unless it was agreed beforehand in writing.

As a rough illustration, if your leave year runs from January to December and you leave at the end of September, you will have built up about nine twelfths of your annual leave. If that comes to more than you have taken, the difference is paid to you. Use the calculator for your real figure, because your contract may work differently.

Notice and garden leave

If you are put on garden leave, you normally stay employed and keep your pay and benefits during your notice period, but you are not expected to work. Check your contract for how it handles holiday during that time. Being paid instead of working your notice normally ends your employment straight away, so check the date your employment ends, because it affects your pay, pension and benefits.

Check your final payslip

  1. Compare it with the breakdown you were given.
  2. Check the holiday days and the daily rate used.
  3. Look at the tax code. A one-off lump sum can sometimes lead to the wrong tax being taken, so read our guide to emergency tax codes.
  4. Make sure you receive your P45.

If it looks wrong

Ask your employer in writing to explain or correct it. Citizens Advice has guidance on getting what you are owed after leaving a job. If it isn’t resolved, contact Acas for free advice. Strict time limits apply to claims, so don’t leave it for long. Our guide on whether your redundancy was fair has the current time limits.
