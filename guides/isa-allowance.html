How the ISA allowance works, and what changed in the 2026 Budget

Last checked: 14 November 2026 · Updated after the Autumn Budget of 28 October 2026

The short answer

In the 2026/27 tax year (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027) you can put up to £20,000 into ISAs in total. That limit is shared across every ISA you pay into, not given separately to each one.

The planned cut to £12,000 for cash ISAs for under-65s from April 2027 has been scrapped. The full £20,000 can still go into cash. The planned 22% charge on cash held in stocks and shares ISAs, and the ban on transferring from them into cash ISAs, were dropped too.

What is changing is tax on savings interest outside an ISA, which rises from 6 April 2027.

One allowance, shared across all your ISAs

GOV.UK says you can save up to £20,000 in one account or split it across several accounts in a tax year. The accounts can be different types: cash ISA, stocks and shares ISA, innovative finance ISA or Lifetime ISA. A Lifetime ISA has its own £4,000 maximum, and that counts towards the £20,000 too.

ExamplePaid in this tax year
Cash ISA£12,000
Stocks and shares ISA£8,000
Total£20,000, so the allowance is used up

If you have already paid money into one ISA this tax year, only what is left of the £20,000 can go into another.

What changed in the 2026 Budget

In the Autumn Budget 2025, the then Chancellor announced three ISA changes to start in April 2027. At the Autumn Budget on 28 October 2026, Chancellor John Healey reversed them.

Planned in 2025Position now
Cash ISA limit cut to £12,000 a year for under-65sScrapped. The limit stays at £20,000 for everyone aged 18 and over.
22% charge on interest on cash held in stocks and shares ISAsScrapped. Cash in a stocks and shares ISA stays tax-free.
Ban on transferring from stocks and shares ISAs into cash ISAsScrapped. You can still transfer between cash and stocks and shares ISAs, and between providers.

These are Budget announcements, so check GOV.UK for the final rules as the legislation is passed.

What is still changing: tax on savings interest

From 6 April 2027, the tax rates on savings interest outside an ISA rise by four percentage points in total. That combines a rise announced in 2025 with a further rise announced in the 2026 Budget.

TaxpayerRate now (2026/27)From April 2027
Basic rate20%24%
Higher rate40%44%
Additional rate45%49%

The Personal Savings Allowance (£1,000 for basic rate taxpayers, £500 for higher rate taxpayers, nothing for additional rate taxpayers) and the £5,000 starting rate for savings are unchanged. The higher rates only apply to interest above those allowances. Interest inside an ISA is tax-free, so the allowance matters more for anyone whose interest outside an ISA is above their allowance.

The allowance does not carry over

The allowance is for one tax year at a time. If you don’t use it by 5 April, you can’t add it to next year’s. The money you have already saved stays in its ISA and stays tax-free; only the right to pay more in is lost.

Taking money out

With most ISAs, money you take out can’t be paid back in without using up allowance again. Some ISAs are “flexible”: GOV.UK says you can take out cash and put it back in the same tax year without reducing that year’s allowance. Your provider can tell you whether your ISA is flexible. If there is a chance you will need the money, check this before you choose an account.

Tax on ISA interest

GOV.UK says you do not pay tax on interest on cash in an ISA or on income or capital gains from investments in an ISA. If you complete a tax return, you do not need to declare ISA interest, income or gains on it.

Common questions about the ISA allowance

Does the interest I earn count towards my allowance?

No. Which? explains that the £20,000 allowance only applies to new money you pay into an ISA in the tax year. Interest, dividends and investment growth inside the ISA don’t use it up, which is why some people hold much more than £20,000 in ISAs over the years.

Can I split my allowance between different ISAs?

Yes. You can pay into more than one ISA in a tax year and divide the £20,000 however you like, as in the example above. The only extra limit is the £4,000 maximum for a Lifetime ISA, which counts towards the £20,000.

What if I want to save more than £20,000?

The part above your allowance has to go somewhere outside an ISA, such as an ordinary savings account or a general investment account (GIA), which is an investment account that isn’t an ISA or a pension. Interest and gains outside an ISA can be taxable, depending on your allowances. Our guide to where to keep cash savings explains the tax on interest.

Do transfers between ISAs use up my allowance?

Not if the transfer is done properly. Ask your new provider to arrange it, because taking money out yourself and paying it back in can use up allowance unless the ISA is flexible.

What was the maximum ISA allowance in earlier years?

Which? says ISAs began in 1999 with a lower allowance, and it reached £20,000 in 2017. It has been £20,000 a tax year since then, including 2021/22. GOV.UK has the figures for older years.

What if I pay in too much?

Contact your provider as soon as you notice. Providers can usually remove the extra amount if the terms allow, and they work with HMRC to put it right.

Which tax year does a payment count towards?

The tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April. A payment that is made after 5 April counts towards the next year’s allowance.
