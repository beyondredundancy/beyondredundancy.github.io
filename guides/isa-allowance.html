How the ISA allowance works, including the April 2027 cash ISA change

Last checked: 25 October 2026 · Covers the 2026/27 tax year

The short answer

In the 2026/27 tax year (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027) you can put up to £20,000 into ISAs in total. That limit is shared across every ISA you pay into, not given separately to each one.

From 6 April 2027, people under 65 will only be able to put up to £12,000 of that £20,000 into cash ISAs. The £20,000 overall limit stays the same.

One allowance, shared across all your ISAs

GOV.UK says you can save up to £20,000 in one account or split it across several accounts in a tax year. The accounts can be different types: cash ISA, stocks and shares ISA, innovative finance ISA or Lifetime ISA. A Lifetime ISA has its own £4,000 maximum, and that counts towards the £20,000 too.

ExamplePaid in this tax year
Cash ISA£12,000
Stocks and shares ISA£8,000
Total£20,000, so the allowance is used up

If you have already paid money into one ISA this tax year, only what is left of the £20,000 can go into another.

What changes in April 2027

The government announced in the November 2025 Budget that the amount that can go into cash ISAs each year will fall to £12,000 from 6 April 2027 for people under 65. The overall £20,000 limit is not changing, so the other £8,000 would need to go into other ISA types, such as a stocks and shares ISA. People aged 65 and over are reported to keep the full £20,000 cash limit.

This means 2026/27 is the last tax year in which someone under 65 can put the full £20,000 into a cash ISA. Check the exact age rules and the current position on GOV.UK before you act, because rules can change.

The allowance does not carry over

The allowance is for one tax year at a time. If you don’t use it by 5 April, you can’t add it to next year’s. The money you have already saved stays in its ISA and stays tax-free; only the right to pay more in is lost.

Taking money out

With most ISAs, money you take out can’t be paid back in without using up allowance again. Some ISAs are “flexible”: GOV.UK says you can take out cash and put it back in the same tax year without reducing that year’s allowance. Your provider can tell you whether your ISA is flexible. If there is a chance you will need the money, check this before you choose an account.

Tax on ISA interest

GOV.UK says you do not pay tax on interest on cash in an ISA or on income or capital gains from investments in an ISA. If you complete a tax return, you do not need to declare ISA interest, income or gains on it.
