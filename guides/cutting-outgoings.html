Cutting your monthly outgoings without panic

Last checked: 25 October 2026

The short answer

Start with the easy savings: things you don’t use and bills you can get cheaper. Protect the bills that matter most, such as rent or mortgage, council tax and energy. If you think you might fall behind on anything, tell the company early, because they can often help, and free debt advice is available.

1. Know your numbers first

You can’t cut what you can’t see. Use our budget planner and look back through 12 months of bank and card statements so you don’t miss annual bills or forgotten subscriptions. It also shows how many months your cash would last, which tells you how urgent any cuts are.

2. Look for the easy savings

  • Subscriptions and memberships you no longer use. You can often pause or cancel them.
  • Regular bills. MoneyHelper says you can often get better deals on regular bills, such as energy, broadband, phone and insurance, and it has a price comparison tool. Before you switch, check when your current contract ends and whether there is an exit fee.
  • Bundles. Check what is included in your phone, broadband and TV packages, and whether you are paying for things you don’t use.
  • Council Tax Reduction. Your local council runs its own scheme and may be able to reduce your bill. See our guide to benefits while job hunting.

3. Be careful what you cut

Some costs are needed to find work, such as a phone, internet access and transport to interviews. Cutting those can make it harder to find a job, so it can be better to cut elsewhere.

4. Know which bills come first

The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) says some debts are more urgent than others because the consequences of not paying can be more serious. These include your mortgage or rent, council tax, and gas or electricity bills. If money is tight, make sure these are covered before less urgent bills such as credit cards or store cards.

5. Tell people early

If you think you might fall behind on a mortgage, loan, rent or bill, contact the company as soon as you can, not after you have missed a payment. The FCA says lenders can offer support, such as changing your mortgage or loan term to make payments more affordable and, in some circumstances, allowing no payments for a temporary period. They should also point you to free debt advice. Landlords and councils can often work with you too if you tell them early.

6. Get free help if you are struggling

If you have missed payments, are juggling several debts or don’t know which to deal with first, MoneyHelper has a debt advice locator that finds free, confidential advice. A debt adviser can help even if you think you have no spare money. Be cautious about borrowing to cover everyday costs, and look for free advice first. Never pay a company for help that is available for free.
