Benefits you may be able to claim while job hunting

Last checked: 25 October 2026 · Covers Great Britain

The short answer

There are two main benefits to look at. New Style Jobseeker’s Allowance is based on your National Insurance record, not on your savings. Universal Credit is based on your household’s money and can include help with housing costs.

You can claim both at the same time. It is worth checking early, even if you have savings, because you may qualify for New Style JSA either way.

New Style Jobseeker’s Allowance

GOV.UK says you may be able to get it if you are unemployed or work less than 16 hours a week on average. It is paid every two weeks, and you can claim it on its own or at the same time as Universal Credit.

  • Who qualifies. You need to have worked as an employee and paid Class 1 National Insurance contributions, usually in the last 2 to 3 years. Class 2 contributions paid by self-employed people don’t count.
  • Savings don’t affect it. It is not means-tested, so savings and a partner’s income don’t change whether you qualify or how much you get.
  • How much. The weekly amount depends on your age. Rates change each April, so check the current figures on GOV.UK.
  • How long. Sources say it can be paid for up to 182 days, which is about six months. When it ends, you may be able to claim Universal Credit.
  • State Pension. GOV.UK says you get National Insurance credits while you claim, which count towards your State Pension.

Universal Credit

Universal Credit is assessed on your household’s income and savings, and it can include an amount for your housing costs. GOV.UK says that having less than £6,000 doesn’t affect your award, that between £6,000 and £16,000 your payments are reduced, and that you can usually only claim if you have no more than £16,000 in money, savings and investments. A redundancy payment counts towards that. Our guide to how much cash to keep accessible has the details.

Claiming

  • Claim online through GOV.UK. It is free, and you should never pay anyone to claim for you.
  • Have these ready: your National Insurance number, your bank details, details of your last employer, and your P45 if you have it.
  • Tell them about any payments you received when you left, such as redundancy pay and pay for your notice period.
  • You will agree what you’ll do to look for work with a work coach at your Jobcentre Plus.

Other help to check

  • Council Tax Reduction. Your local council runs its own scheme, so ask them.
  • A free benefits check. The charity Turn2us has a benefits calculator and a redundancy page that explain what you might be able to claim.
  • Help finding work. See our checklist on what to check in your redundancy offer for the Jobcentre Plus Rapid Response Service.
